# TLM-060: Independent Legal Limits

This lesson keeps copyright permission, restriction, or uncertainty separate from other legal regimes. It never treats fair use, an educational exception, lawful acquisition, or a computational copyright exception as permission to bypass access controls, access a protected computer without authorization, intercept communications, process personal data without a demonstrated basis, breach contract or confidentiality duties, or improperly acquire, use, or disclose trade secrets.

## Bounded evaluator

`evaluateIndependentLimits` accepts an already-produced copyright evaluation plus explicit tri-state facts. It returns a separate assessment for:

- 17 U.S.C. § 1201 access controls, including unresolved narrow-exception facts;
- 18 U.S.C. § 1030 protected-computer access;
- 18 U.S.C. § 2511 communications interception;
- personal-data processing, without inventing a single nationwide privacy rule;
- contract and confidentiality evidence, without deciding governing law or enforceability; and
- 18 U.S.C. § 1836 trade-secret claims and improper acquisition, use, or disclosure.

An indicated limit does not itself establish liability. Unknown elements stay unresolved. A complete negative fixture says only that the fixture does not indicate an independent limit; it is not a general declaration of lawfulness.

## Source and scope boundary

The catalog is a dated learning reference, not a certified current-law service or legal advice. Section 1201(f) is a narrow interoperability provision, not a general circumvention authorization. The federal access, interception, and trade-secret statutes each require their own elements, exceptions, jurisdiction, and remedies. Contract, confidentiality, and privacy questions require separate terms, governing-law, jurisdiction, authorization, and factual evidence.
