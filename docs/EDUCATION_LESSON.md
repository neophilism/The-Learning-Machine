# TLM-059: Teaching settings and present-law boundaries

`evaluateEducationLesson` compares nonprofit face-to-face classroom teaching, private tutoring, for-profit teaching, live transmission, recording, and copying without converting “educational” into an automatic legal conclusion.

The narrow `17 U.S.C. § 110(1)` result is returned only for a complete fixture involving a performance or display by instructors or pupils, face-to-face, at a nonprofit educational institution, in a classroom or similar instructional place, with the statute's audiovisual-copy and responsible-person knowledge condition satisfied. A missing condition means that this particular exemption is not established; it does not declare infringement or decide authorization, fair use, another part of § 110, or another exception.

Fair use always remains a separate four-factor analysis. A public performance or display can implicate § 106 even when it is educational. Live transmission and recorded instruction are not silently treated as face-to-face teaching. The repository's proposed-bill record contains no authoritative full text, so the lesson identifies the proposed educational expansion as unevaluable and never presents it as enacted law.
